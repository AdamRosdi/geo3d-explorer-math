Common Year 6 Misconception! Key Concept

Pupils often mistakenly add the full surface area of Solid A + Solid B (236 + 90 = 326 cm²). When 3D shapes are joined together, the contact interface faces are hidden inside and are NO LONGER part of the outer surface!

Contact Area Hidden = 2 faces × 25 cm² = 50 cm²
- 50 cm²

Mathematical Reasoning

Live Calculation
General Formula:
Total SA = SA(Cuboid) + SA(Pyramid) - (2 × Contact Area)
Solid A: Rectangular Cuboid
2 × (8×5 + 8×6 + 5×6) = 2 × (40 + 48 + 30)
236 cm²
Solid B: Square Pyramid
Base (5×5) + 4 Triangles (½ × 5 × 6.5) = 25 + 65
90 cm²
Contact Interface Area (Overlapping)
5 cm × 5 cm = 25 cm² (2 faces = 50 cm²)
- 50 cm²
Correct Total Surface Area Attached
236 + 90 - 50 =
276 cm²

Year 6 Lesson Guide: Composite 3D Shapes

KSSR Semakan Mathematics Topic 6.0 Space

1. What is a Composite 3D Shape?

A composite 3D shape is formed by attaching two or more simple solids together. In this lesson, we attach a Square Pyramid on top of a Rectangular Cuboid.

2. Use Slider 1: Separate Solids

Pull the 'Separate Solids' slider to lift the pyramid up. Notice the bright red/magenta faces on both solids! That is the overlapping contact interface (5cm x 5cm = 25 cm²).

3. Use Slider 2: 2D Net Unfolding

Pull the '2D Net Unfolding' slider to flatten all exposed faces. Count all exposed faces and observe how the contact face is excluded from outer surface calculation!
